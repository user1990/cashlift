CRAP1 calculator

Explore the interaction between cyclomatic complexity and coverage. Use the score to prioritize investigation, never to certify quality.

10.5Below the original CRAP1 warning threshold
CRAP(m) = comp(m)² × (1 − coverage(m) / 100)³ + comp(m)

How to read it

PatternQuestionCandidate response
Low complexity, low coverageIs the behavior valuable or dead?Add a meaningful test or delete it.
High complexity, high coverageIs change still difficult despite the safety net?Consider behavior-preserving refactoring.
High complexity, low coverageCan you characterize behavior safely?Add characterization tests, then simplify.
Low score, recurring defectsWhat does the metric not see?Inspect requirements, state, concurrency, integration, or security.

Comparability warning

The original formula used basis-path coverage. Implementations may substitute branch, line, or statement coverage, and complexity tools count constructs differently. Scores from different tools are not automatically comparable.

Threshold context

The original author proposed scores over 30 as a warning heuristic. Robert Martin’s 4/6/8 discussion in the supplied interview is a personal experiment conducted at or near full coverage, where CRAP collapses to complexity. Neither is a universal engineering standard.
