Turn claims into evidence

Your win: rewrite one generic frontend bullet so a hiring panel can verify ownership, mechanism, and consequence.

  • 12 minutes
  • Resume skill
  • Immediate feedback

The central distinction

A technology is not evidence. “Used React and Playwright” names tools; it does not tell the reader what problem you owned, why your decision worked, or what changed.

Model

Context → Action → Mechanism → Outcome → Scope

A strong claim lets the reader reconstruct this chain. Scope can substitute for confidential metrics, but vague praise cannot.

Retrieval check

Which bullet provides the strongest evidence?

Why the third option still needs a second sentence

It is the best start because it names an owned mechanism. A finished bullet still needs consequence and scope: “Reworked checkout state and recoverable error handling across five markets, reducing failed-purchase retries by 18%.”

Practical example

Weak

Worked on a Next.js migration and improved performance.

Stronger

Led an incremental migration of 40,000 content pages to Next.js, using route-level rollout checks and real-user monitoring; cut bounce rate 46% and improved conversion 5% during validation.

The stronger version names action, rollout mechanism, scope, and outcomes. Never borrow its numbers: use only results you can explain under questioning. A real Next.js performance case study from QuintoAndar reported comparable categories of evidence, demonstrating why a measurement method matters.

Your turn

Rewrite: “Used React, TypeScript, Playwright, and Sentry to improve quality.”

Model: Established release checks for checkout and authentication journeys using Playwright traces and Sentry release monitoring, reducing escaped critical regressions across three applications.

Audit it: Does your version include a real mechanism? A verifiable result or scope? Your exact contribution?

Transfer question

In an interview, what would make “reduced regressions” credible? Answer before opening.

Answer

Define the incident class, baseline window, comparison window, data source, and your contribution. A Staff-caliber answer also explains adoption across teams and what guardrail remained.

Primary reading: Farfetch: linking real-user performance to business KPIs. It models instrumentation, careful correlation, and cross-functional adoption.

Ask your agent about any evidence chain that feels ambiguous.
