Lesson 5 of 6

Connect engineering to impact—without fiction

Your win: select a credible impact claim and explain the measurement design behind it.

  • 14 minutes
  • Business judgment
  • Attribution drill

The evidence ladder

  1. Causal experiment: randomized or controlled rollout.
  2. Strong comparison: before/after with confounders addressed.
  3. Operational association: metric moved alongside the change.
  4. Scope evidence: adoption, systems, markets, teams, users.
  5. Activity: implementation only.

Do not present correlation as causation. A seasonal sales increase after a performance release does not prove the release caused it. Controlled rollout, segmented analysis, or a carefully chosen relative metric strengthens the claim.

Credibility check

Which statement is strongest?

Finished evidence

Led checkout rendering and error-recovery changes validated through a staged experiment; the treatment cohort completed purchase 6% more often, with no increase in duplicate-order or support-contact rates.

When exact metrics are unavailable

Scope is legitimate evidence: “shared authentication architecture adopted by three applications and four teams.” Add the capability or risk reduced. Do not fabricate a percentage because it looks stronger.

Attribution drill

Your team shipped a faster checkout in November. Conversion rose 9%, but marketing also launched a holiday campaign. Write the strongest honest resume claim.

Without cohort evidence: Led checkout performance improvements that reduced median interaction latency by 35% before peak season; conversion rose 9% in the same period, though a concurrent campaign prevented clean attribution.

With controlled evidence: Led checkout performance improvements; a staged cohort comparison isolated a 4% completion lift after controlling for campaign traffic.

Metric defense questions

What was the baseline?

Name the window, population, device or market segment, and metric definition.

What else changed?

Identify releases, campaigns, pricing, seasonality, traffic mix, and instrumentation changes.

What guardrail protected against harm?

Examples: error rate, refunds, support contacts, accessibility regressions, or page stability.

Primary reading: Swappie’s performance case study, especially its relative mobile conversion metric. Contrast with Farfetch’s RUM-to-business analysis.

Ask your agent to stress-test any metric before placing it on a resume.
