Recognize Staff-shaped work

Your win: distinguish hard Senior delivery from cross-team leverage without using job titles as evidence.

  • 14 minutes
  • Level calibration
  • Scenario choice

Same skills, different center of gravity

Staff engineers still code, coordinate, and mentor. The difference is what those activities serve. Senior engineers typically own complex domain outcomes. Staff engineers shape technical direction, resolve systemic constraints, and improve how multiple teams make decisions.

Scope × leverage × durability is a better level signal than project size.

  • Scope: how many systems, teams, journeys, or markets?
  • Leverage: did others become more effective?
  • Durability: did a capability or decision system remain?

Level the scenario

Which is strongest Staff evidence?

Scenario detail

The migration leader documented decision criteria, piloted with one team, created compatibility adapters, aligned three engineering leads, and measured adoption and failure rates across four applications. Teams could retain local implementations when constraints justified them.

Centralization is not leadership

Forcing every team into one abstraction can increase coupling and migration risk. Staff judgment creates sensible defaults and explicit escape hatches. The goal is not uniformity; it is better system-wide outcomes.

Practical drill

Upgrade this Senior story into a credible Staff story without inflating it:

Designed the authentication architecture for my application.

Possible answer: Identified inconsistent session and error behavior across three applications, aligned product and platform owners on a shared auth contract, piloted compatibility adapters, and led staged adoption with escape criteria; teams gained consistent telemetry and recovery flows without a synchronized rewrite.

This is Staff-shaped only if those events actually happened.

Interview questions and answers

“How did you influence without authority?”

Strong answers show discovery, stakeholder incentives, written trade-offs, a low-risk pilot, dissent handling, and evidence that teams voluntarily adopted or adapted the direction.

“What would have happened without you?”

Avoid heroic claims. Describe the systemic gap you made visible, the coordination you supplied, and the decision or capability that became durable.
