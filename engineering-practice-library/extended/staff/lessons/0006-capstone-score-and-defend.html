Score the evidence, then defend it

Your win: produce a weighted diagnosis of a resume and convert its largest gap into an interview-ready evidence story.

  • 25 minutes
  • Capstone
  • Score calculator

Score only demonstrated evidence

A resume does not receive points for experience the reviewer must assume. Score what is visible and credible. A candidate may be stronger than the document; that means the resume has an evidence gap.

Do not optimize the smallest gap

Multiply the missing points by relevance to the target role and recoverability from real experience. Missing payment ownership in a checkout role matters more than a certification. A buried cross-team migration may be recoverable by rewriting; nonexistent experience is not.

Capstone story

Take the lowest high-weight category. Write a five-part evidence chain and prepare answers to the defense questions.

Defense questions

  1. What was your decision, specifically?
  2. Which alternative did you reject, and why?
  3. How did you measure the result?
  4. What failed or surprised you?
  5. How did other teams adopt the outcome?

A strong answer makes contribution boundaries explicit, names a real trade-off, includes a failure or revision, distinguishes causal from scope evidence, and shows durable leverage. If it sounds frictionless, it is probably too abstract.

Completion standard

You have completed the course when you can score a real resume, cite evidence for every category, name the three largest gaps, rewrite two bullets, and defend one Staff-shaped story under follow-up questioning. Exposure alone is not mastery.

Primary reading: Revisit the GitLab Staff Frontend framework and compare it with your scored evidence—not your title.

Send your score, rewritten bullets, and one story to your agent for adversarial feedback. A learning record should be created only after you demonstrate the skill.
