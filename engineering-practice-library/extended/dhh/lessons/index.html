The human loop

The agent can do more of the implementation. You own the direction, the taste, the decision, and the stop condition.

DescribeState the problem and desired outcome.
GenerateLet the agent choose a path.
Use itInteract with something real.
CompareGive yourself a few options.
RefinePush for simpler, better shape.
VerifyRun tests and inspect risk.
ShipMake the result useful to someone.
The role change: less time translating a human plan into code; more time deciding what deserves to exist and where it should go.
Conversation map

22 ideas worth keeping

Compressed from the major chapters of the conversation. They are DHH’s claims and examples, not independent fact checks.

01 · TIME

Progress arrives in bursts

AI moved from autocomplete to agents that can use tools, check work, and complete meaningful tasks in months.

Notice the discontinuity
02 · ORGANISATIONS

Implementation is rarely the bottleneck

Human bandwidth, communication, vision, and taste often limit established teams more than their ability to write code.

Shorten the chain
03 · SCOPE

Build the 5% you actually use

A focused replacement for the small part of a large product you need can be within one person’s reach.

Make the personal tool
04 · OPEN SOURCE

Agents widen the commons

People who could not previously contribute can now turn good ideas into pull requests; maintainers can filter the flood.

Keep the human merge
05 · LANGUAGE

Vibe coding is not programming

For DHH, vibe coding means asking an agent to build software without inspecting the implementation. Agent-accelerated work keeps judgment in the loop.

Know what you are delegating
06 · PRODUCT

Software is product management

Who is it for? What should it do? What comes first? These choices become more valuable when implementation gets cheap.

Direct the outcome
07 · DISCOVERY

Do not overspecify the first version

Start with a useful, imperfect manifestation. You discover what you want by using the thing, not by completing a perfect upfront spec.

Make → use → learn
08 · TASTE

Use differential evaluation

Three concrete options make taste visible quickly; too many options create paralysis. Compare, choose, and explain only afterward.

Three beats twenty-two
09 · ARCHITECTURE

Speed does not cancel shape

Many individually reasonable changes can destroy a coherent system. Agents still need feedback on simplicity, proportion, boundaries, and critical logic.

Review the shape
10 · QUALITY

Beautiful code still has a job

Its economic payoff is changing, but coherent systems are easier for agents to understand, evolve, and operate within scarce token budgets.

Reduce search cost
11 · PARALLELISM

One agent can feel slow

Multiple agents and multiple machines turn waiting into a stream of decisions, unblockings, reviews, and next tasks.

Create a decision queue
12 · CONTEXT

Voice can be a design medium

Long, stream-of-consciousness voice prompts can capture early design thinking—if transcription is cleaned with a domain-aware dictionary.

Let the idea breathe
13 · SPEED

A bold metric creates discovery

Chasing a numerical target such as a faster install exposes hidden opportunities: preloading, parallel work, smaller packages, and better defaults.

Pick a number
14 · MODELS

There is no permanent winner

Different models and harnesses have different strengths. Competition and open-weight alternatives matter because the frontier keeps moving.

Use the right tool
15 · SECURITY

Offense and defense share capability

Agents can discover complex vulnerability chains humans may miss, creating urgent patching work and stronger systems—if teams actually look.

Verify harder than before
16 · LINUX

Old friction can become leverage

Config files, command-line tools, openness, and malleability make Linux unusually friendly to agents that can inspect and change the whole system.

Make the system yours
17 · PLUGINS

Personal software can become a platform

Give agents a clear extension contract and ordinary users can create meaningful features for themselves and for other people.

Design for extension
18 · WORK

Builders are not the same as typists

Mechanical programming work is threatened; people who love building, choosing, and creating may find more room to operate.

Move toward creation
19 · ATTENTION

Mission prevents overwhelm

Power without a destination becomes multitasking and burnout. A singular outcome gives the new capability somewhere to go.

Choose the destination
20 · SOCIAL LIFE

Do not let algorithms choose your mood

Engagement systems amplify what is enraging and addictive. Breaks, deliberate feeds, travel, and real people restore perspective.

Protect the channel
21 · DISAGREEMENT

Difference is not incompatibility

DHH argues that people can disagree about politics, immigration, or culture and still collaborate around shared interests and work.

Keep the conversation human
22 · FINITUDE

Progress and mortality belong together

He pairs optimism about the future with a reminder to live now, resist total optimisation, value family, and remember that time is finite.

Memento mori
Reported experiments

The ideas in motion

These examples make the philosophy concrete: a narrow problem, a fast feedback loop, and a result that changes what feels possible.

Omawrite: the personal 5%

Typora had more features than DHH needed on Linux. He asked for a C++/Qt writing tool, got a first version in about 20 minutes, and switched fully within two days.

Omarchy Quattro: the agent-led release

DHH says he hand-wrote none of the new shipped code, while reviewing the system’s shape and critical model-layer lines. He describes the release as a team victory.

Open source: sort chaff, surface pearls

He reports merging more than 1,000 pull requests in three months, with agents reviewing and filtering submissions before the human merge decision.

Speed: make the target visible

Omarchy’s install target moved from two minutes to under one minute. Preloading during user input and shrinking the image helped turn speed into a discovery game.

Translation: a task, not a language barrier

An agent translated a Python terminal-effects library to dependency-free Rust in under 45 minutes, according to DHH, reducing startup time and execution cost.

Crash watcher: diagnosis becomes ambient

In his description, a crash triggers an agent to inspect logs and source, identify the likely failing line, and prepare a detailed bug report.

Numbers and outcomes in this section are reported in the conversation and have not been independently verified here.

A repeatable practice

The DHH-inspired playbook

Use this when you want to turn a fuzzy idea into a working artifact without losing judgment.

Pick one real problem

Choose something you understand because you already live with the friction.

Describe the outcome

State who it helps, what “better” looks like, and the constraints that matter.

Ask for a rough version

Let the agent manifest something quickly; do not try to finish the spec first.

Run several paths

Use parallel agents or alternative designs when the decision benefits from comparison.

Use the artifact

Dogfood the result. Your real reactions are better product evidence than abstract debate.

Push for simpler shape

Ask for less complexity, inspect architecture, and keep change local where possible.

Verify the dangerous parts

Make the agent test its work, then review security, data, failure paths, and critical logic.

Ship or share

Package the useful 5%, document it, and let other people decide whether it helps them too.

Beyond the keyboard

What the future does not replace

The conversation ends where it began: with people, meaning, and the choice to use power deliberately.

Build with ambition

Small teams can pursue goals that once required large organisations. A bold target can pull better ideas out of the system.

Keep real life real

Take breaks from feeds, spend time with people, and resist turning every health or social choice into an optimisation problem.

Have compassion during change

Productivity can create abundance while causing real pain for individuals. Progress and empathy are not competing requirements.

Closing principle: More power means more responsibility for direction. Focus on what you can build today.
