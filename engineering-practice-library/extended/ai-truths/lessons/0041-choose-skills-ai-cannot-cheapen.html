Lesson 41

Choose skills AI cannot easily cheapen

Staff-level point: In an AI market, the valuable skill is rarely raw execution. It is owning the outcome, the trust boundary, and the feedback loop.

Real Product / Engineering Example

A team says it needs "AI automation." A junior build ships a Zapier flow. A staff-level build maps the business process, identifies failure states, stores workflow state, adds human approval for risky steps, tracks outcome metrics, and creates a rollback path.

Practice Drill

Pick one skill from the reference card. Write its current low-value execution version, then rewrite it as a system-level capability with owner, metric, verification, and buyer value.

Operating Checklist

  • Separate execution from judgment. AI cheapens execution first.
  • Find the buyer's real reason to pay: revenue, risk, time, trust, or leverage.
  • Identify which parts a model can do now and which parts require accountable ownership.
  • Turn the skill into a repeatable system: inputs, tools, state, quality checks, approvals, and outputs.
  • Require a proof loop before investing months into the skill.

Advanced Lesson

The transcript ranks skills by barrier of entry, defensibility, and profitability. That is useful, but incomplete for experienced builders. Add a fourth lens: system leverage. A skill becomes more durable when it can own a workflow, create reusable assets, compound data, or improve a measurable business loop.

For example, "prompt engineering" alone is easy to copy. But context engineering plus evals plus tool orchestration becomes an operating system for repeated work. "Software development" alone gets cheaper. But product engineering that ships tested, observable, secure workflows remains scarce.

Feedback Loop

Pass condition: You can explain why the upgraded version of the skill remains valuable even when model output gets cheaper.

Quick retrieval: Which version is most defensible?
