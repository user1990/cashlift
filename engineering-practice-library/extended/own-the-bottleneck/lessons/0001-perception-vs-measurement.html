Lesson 01 · Signal

Predicted speed is not shipped speed

After an AI session, log what you expected, what it felt like, and what the clock actually showed. The gap is the curriculum.

Perception → measurementOne retrieval checkNo setup required

Win for this lesson

You can separate predicted time, felt time, and measured time for one real task, then name which pipeline step actually grew.

Do not clash

This lesson does not teach how to write tests, read coverage, or run mutation testing. Those live in Tests as evidence. Here you only measure whether generation speed became throughput.

Teams often report that agents write code faster. That can be true locally and still false as delivery. Predicted speed, felt speed, and measured elapsed time can disagree. Treat vendor claims and your own afterglow the same way: as hypotheses.

Three clocks

P

Predicted

What you believed before the session: “this will be about 24% faster.” Prediction is useful; it is not evidence.

F

Felt

What you believed after: “it felt quicker.” Fluency and a large diff create a sense of progress even when review and rework expand.

M

Measured

Wall-clock from first prompt to a mergeable, reviewed change—or to an explicit stop. Include review, tests, and the “almost right” remainder.

Independent studies of experienced developers on familiar, mature repositories have shown this split: people expected and felt a speedup while measured completion time did not match the feeling. Use the method. Do not freeze any one percentage as a permanent law.

Retrieval check

Which number should you use to decide whether AI made this task faster?

Make it yours

Complete one AI-assisted task today. Before you start, write predicted minutes. After you stop, write felt minutes and measured minutes. Name the step that grew: design, generation, review, verification, or waiting on someone else.

Primary research

Read the study for the perception-versus-measurement method on mature codebases. Cite the method in your notes; do not treat a single headline percentage as the course answer.
