Lesson 05 · Users

Coding can be how you avoid the hard step

When generation is cheap, the graveyard fills faster. A working weekend project that nobody used is inventory, not a career asset.

Ship to a userOne retrieval checkEmotional friction

Win for this lesson

After an MVP exists, you can name the next user-facing action and refuse another feature commit until that action happens.

Do not clash

This is not a marketing course and not DHH’s full build loop (DHH's Agentic Era). DHH covers generate → use → compare. This lesson isolates the emotional dodge: more features instead of a user.

For solo work, the bottleneck is rarely syntax. It is putting something in front of a person who can reject it. Generation makes the dodge cheaper: you can add another screen in an hour and still not have a conversation.

Inventory vs delivery

I

Inventory

Repos, demos, and nearly-ready MVPs that never met a user. They feel like progress because they are visible.

D

Delivery

A person used it. You have a note: what they tried, what broke, what they asked for. That note is the next spec.

Rule for this course: once you have a path a stranger can click, the next commit is forbidden until one real person has seen it. A teammate counts. A stranger is better. Your future self does not count.

Retrieval check

Your MVP runs locally. What is the highest-value next move?

Make it yours

Name one project that has lingered. Write the smallest user-facing action you can take in 48 hours. After you take it, record what they did—not what you hope they thought.
